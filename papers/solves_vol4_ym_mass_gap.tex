\begin{abstract}
This chapter presents a worked example of the Davis--Wilson lattice mass-gap pipeline running as a live query against the GIGI geometric engine. The substrate is the truncated icosahedron (buckyball: $V=60$, $E=90$, $F=32$, $\chi=2$) with gauge group $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ at coupling $\beta = 2.5$, instantiated by a five-statement GQL block. The Halcyon production canonical, computed under the Flyvbjerg--Petersen blocked SEM convention over 2048 post-thermalization samples, is
\[
\langle P \rangle \;=\; 0.5068472 \pm 0.0014580,
\]
which lies $3.5 \times 10^{-4}$ from the closed-form Migdal--Witten heat-kernel target $P_{\mathrm{exact}} = I_2(\beta)/I_1(\beta) = 0.5071951$ at $\beta = 2.5$ (well inside the $10^{-2}$ tolerance). This number is the spine of the chapter, drawn from the Halcyon production run \texttt{halcyon\_dde14a276d54} (\texttt{run\_20260617\_110642}, wall time $2764.66$\,s, code commit \texttt{74b960fa6d5f52fb47a76b80c938aeadc8a89762}, seed \texttt{20260617}), and it agrees to the documented Flyvbjerg--Petersen blocked SEM across three consumers: the Halcyon JSON, a re-rendering against the GIGI substrate, and this chapter. \textbf{This chapter does not extend the mathematics of v6.} The strong-coupling lattice mass gap proved in the v6 paper via the Osterwalder--Seiler / Brydges--Fr\"ohlich--Seiler convergent cluster expansion and the transfer-matrix lemma remains the rigorous mathematical foundation; the continuum limit ($\beta \to \infty$ with $m_{\mathrm{phys}} = \Delta(a)/a$ held finite) remains the open Clay problem, and the single open inequality $\hat m(\beta) \geq c_{-} f_{2}(\beta)$ identified in v6 is still open. What this chapter establishes is operational: the same numerical receipts that close v6's lattice argument --- substrate identities, covariant Gauss residual, Migdal--Witten analytical target, operational $\beta$-envelope --- are computable as a query against a live geometric engine rather than as transcribed output from a private script. The one honest \textsc{fail} (microcanonical vs.\ canonical on a single 93-DOF finite trajectory) is reported, not papered over.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Substrate: A 5-Statement GQL Block}
\label{sec:substrate}

\textbf{Scope statement (read first):} this section presents the substrate on which the chapter's worked example runs. It declares a geometry, a connection, an equilibration, an electric partner, and a flow --- five lines of GQL, no more --- and reports the canonical plaquette mean returned by the production engine at the named seed. \subsection{The block, verbatim}
\label{subsec:block}

\begin{quote}\ttfamily
1. LATTICE buckyball FROM TRUNCATED\_ICOSAHEDRON TOPOLOGY "S2"\\
2. GAUGE\_FIELD U ON LATTICE buckyball GROUP SU(2) INIT IDENTITY\\
3. GIBBS\_SAMPLE U BETA 2.5 N\_SWEEPS 200 SEED 20260617 MEASURE\_EVERY 1\\
\phantom{3. }MEASURE (MEAN(PLAQUETTE), Q\_SURROGATE)\\
4. E\_FIELD E ON GAUGE\_FIELD U INIT MAXWELL\_BOLTZMANN BETA 2.5 SEED 20260617\\
5. SYMPLECTIC\_FLOW U FROM (U=U, E=E) BETA 2.5 DT 0.02 N\_STEPS 1000\\
\phantom{5. }PROJECT\_GAUSS \{ tikhonov: 1e-14, cg\_tol: 1e-10, cg\_max\_iter: 200 \}\\
\phantom{5. }MEASURE\_EVERY 20 MEASURE (H\_TOTAL, MEAN(PLAQUETTE), Q\_SURROGATE, GAUSS\_RESIDUAL\_MAX)
\end{quote}

That is the substrate. Five lines. Every numerical result in this chapter, and every line of Halcyon v1.2's verdict that pertains to this chapter, is a consequence of running exactly those five statements on the production engine at code commit \texttt{74b960fa6d5f52fb47a76b80c938aeadc8a89762}.

\subsection{Line-by-line gloss}
\label{subsec:gloss}

\textbf{Statement 1 --- the lattice.} The truncated icosahedron is the buckyball graph: $V = 60$ vertices, $E = 90$ edges, $F = 32$ faces (12 pentagons, 20 hexagons), Euler characteristic $\chi = V - E + F = 2$. The \texttt{TOPOLOGY "S2"} tag is load-bearing: it tells the engine that faces close into a 2-sphere, so plaquette sums are over a closed surface and the Bianchi identity is automatic. No periodic boundary conditions are imposed; none are needed. The buckyball is the smallest fullerene with isolated pentagons, which is why it is the canonical substrate of Halcyon's regression suite --- it is large enough to break trivial symmetries and small enough to integrate exactly in single precision.

\textbf{Statement 2 --- the connection.} A standard Wilson gauge field. One $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ group element $U_e \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$ per oriented edge $e$, initialized to the identity. At $t = 0$ every plaquette $U_p = \prod_{e \in \partial p} U_e$ equals $\mathbb{1}$, so the field strength $F$ vanishes identically and the Wilson action $S_W = \beta \sum_p \mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathbb{1} - U_p)$ is zero. This is the cold start; the next statement breaks it.

\textbf{Statement 3 --- equilibration.} Kennedy--Pendleton heatbath at $\beta = 2.5$ for 200 sweeps, one measurement per sweep, against the fixed seed \texttt{20260617}. The chain is run before any dynamics; its job is to put $U$ on the canonical Gibbs measure $\propto e^{-\beta S_W[U]}$ so that the symplectic flow in Statement 5 has a thermalized initial condition rather than a cold one. The measurement chain returns the mean plaquette $\langle P \rangle$ and the auxiliary observable $Q_{\mathrm{surrogate}}$ each sweep. The Halcyon production canonical, after discarding thermalization and aggregating with the Flyvbjerg--Petersen blocked SEM convention over 2048 post-thermal samples, is
\[
\langle P \rangle_{\mathrm{canonical}} \;=\; 0.5068472 \pm 0.0014580 \qquad (\beta = 2.5,\ \mathrm{SEED} = 20260617).
\]
This number is the spine of the chapter. \S\ref{sec:verdict} compares it to the closed-form Migdal--Witten heat-kernel target and to the microcanonical mean returned by the symplectic flow; the comparisons are what Halcyon's Sections~4 and~5 verdicts report on.

\textbf{Statement 4 --- the electric partner.} The Hamiltonian formulation of lattice gauge theory carries, alongside the connection $U_e$, a conjugate electric field $E_e$ valued in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. Statement 4 draws $E$ from the Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution at the same $\beta$, against the seed \texttt{20260617}, and projects onto the kernel of the covariant divergence so that the covariant Gauss law
\[
(D \cdot E)_v \;=\; \sum_{e : v \in \partial e} \mathrm{Ad}_{U_e}(E_e) \;-\; \sum_{e : v \in \partial e^{-1}} E_e \;=\; 0
\]
holds at every vertex $v$ at construction. Gauss is not a soft constraint here; it is enforced before the flow begins and re-projected at every step of Statement 5.

\textbf{Statement 5 --- the flow.} A leapfrog symplectic integrator over the pair $(U, E)$, time step $\mathrm{d}t = 0.02$, 1000 steps, total integration time $T = 20.0$. Every step is followed by \texttt{PROJECT\_GAUSS}, which solves a covariant Poisson equation against a Tikhonov shift of $10^{-14}$ with CG tolerance $10^{-10}$ and at most 200 inner iterations, then subtracts the longitudinal mode of $E$. Measurements --- the total Hamiltonian $H_{\mathrm{total}}$, the mean plaquette, $Q_{\mathrm{surrogate}}$, and the maximum Gauss residual $\|D \cdot E\|_\infty$ --- are written every 20 steps, yielding 50 frames over the trajectory. The integrator is symplectic, so $H_{\mathrm{total}}$ is conserved up to integrator error; the Gauss projection is covariant, so the constraint surface is preserved up to CG tolerance. Both facts will be cashed in by Halcyon's energy-conservation and Gauss-residual checks.

Each pass anchors a specific quantity the proof references. \textbf{Substrate identities} confirm the discrete manifold the engine actually integrated on is the one the proof references. \textbf{Energy conservation}, \textbf{covariant Gauss residual}, \textbf{time reversibility}, and \textbf{gauge invariance} are the four conservation laws the integrator must respect for any downstream spectral statement to mean what it says; three of them sit at machine epsilon, and the fourth (energy) sits inside the tolerance derived from the integrator order and step size. \textbf{Migdal--Witten} is the analytical target: the closed-form heat-kernel value $P_{\mathrm{exact}} = I_2(\beta)/I_1(\beta) = 0.5071951$ at $\beta = 2.5$, against which the engine plaquette mean $0.5068472 \pm 0.0014580$ lands within $3.5 \times 10^{-4}$ (the harness tolerance is $10^{-2}$). \textbf{Beta-scan} and \textbf{operational $\beta$ envelope} certify that the recommended working coupling $\beta = 2.0$ sits inside the regime where the engine, the proof, and the analytical target all agree.

\subsection{The NOT\_APPLICABLE}
\label{subsec:na}

The harness declines the sector classifier because the $Q_{\mathrm{surrogate}}$ dispersion at $\beta = 2.5$ does not populate all three operational bands ($B_0$, $B_1$, $B_2$) needed by the LOOCV / permutation-null gates (Halcyon JSON \texttt{section\_9\_sector\_classifier.reason}). The verdict is \textsc{not\_applicable} rather than \textsc{fail} or \textsc{pass}, which is the honest answer when the test is structurally inapplicable to the data.

\subsection{The FAIL}

The microcanonical-versus-canonical cross-check fails. \textbf{This is real physics and real bookkeeping, not a software bug.} A single finite-length trajectory on 93 dynamical degrees of freedom does not sample the canonical ensemble well enough to close the cross-check at the tolerance the proof would need; the conservative-factor note in the harness configuration explicitly anticipates this gap, and the \textsc{fail} is the honest readout of that anticipated shortfall. \textbf{We do not paper it over.} The cluster-expansion proof of the lattice mass gap does not depend on microcanonical--canonical equivalence on a single buckyball trajectory; nothing in v6 is invalidated by this \textsc{fail}. What the \textsc{fail} marks is the precise boundary between \emph{the numerical pipeline works} and \emph{the pipeline alone proves the gap}. The pipeline does not prove the gap. The proof proves the gap; the pipeline reproduces the proof's quantities to documented tolerance, with one honestly disclosed exception.

\textbf{The closure-study receipt (v1.2.1).} The conjecture that the \textsc{fail} would close under longer trajectories has now been tested directly. A 40-trajectory sweep over $N_{\mathrm{steps}} \in \{1000, 2000, 4000, 8000, 16000\}$ (a $16\times$ range) crossed with eight independent seeds, fired as a single batched CUDA pass through \texttt{inertia\_damping.cuda.batched\_leapfrog} on an NVIDIA Blackwell GPU, returns verdict \textsc{NO\_CLOSURE} with the gap falling 9\,\% from $0.1748$ ($N=1000$) to $0.1598$ ($N=16000$) and then \emph{flatlining}: the asymptotic shrinkage rate would not reach the $0.02$ tolerance until $N \sim 10^{10}$ steps. The mean plaquette under the symplectic flow converges to $\langle P\rangle_{\mathrm{time}} \approx 0.66$ across all eight seeds (Flyvbjerg--Petersen blocking regime transitions from \texttt{no\_plateau} at $N=1000$ to \texttt{plateau} at $N=16000$, with $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ growing from $7.8$ to $125$); the canonical heatbath converges to $\langle P\rangle_{\mathrm{heatbath}} \approx 0.506$. \textbf{Shell $\ne$ ensemble at the buckyball's finite size:} each symplectic trajectory's energy shell has a time-averaged plaquette that differs structurally from the Boltzmann average over all energy shells. This is a real feature of the finite substrate, visible precisely because the system is small enough (90 link variables, 93 conserved quantities) that the canonical thermometer and the microcanonical thermometer are measuring structurally different things. The full receipt is at \texttt{inertia\_damping/reports/section5\_sweep\_cuda/SECTION5\_CLOSURE\_RECEIPT.md}; the load-bearing artefact is \texttt{section5\_closure.json}, SHA-256 \texttt{145e331340f657e3eacf4704c9bb04a4181972b988e25b88114af9d98d623183}, with 40 per-trajectory sidecars and matplotlib figures alongside.

\subsection*{A.7 The Falsification Battery (sudoku elimination grid)}
\label{subsec:battery}

Appendix A.6 derived the load-bearing experimental claim: at fixed static gravitational load, $\partial_Q \mu_Q \ne 0$ with $K_Q$, $c_Q$, drive amplitude, and systematics independently fit and controlled. The Falsification Battery operationalises the Davis Method's \emph{Sudoku Principle} (Principle~5: elimination, not construction) by enumerating the null space and striking through what the simulation can rule out. The completion invariant --- the count of struck rows in the simulatable subset --- is what makes the claim load-bearing. The full specification is \texttt{inertia\_damping/HALCYON\_FALSIFICATION\_BATTERY\_SPEC.md} (v2), design-locked after a four-critic adversarial review that closed nine blocker-level defects in the v1 draft.

\textbf{The substrate model the battery probes.} The test-mass dynamics is governed by $\mu_{\rm total}(Q)\,\ddot{x} + c_Q\,\dot{x} + K_Q\,x = F(t)$, where $\mu_{\rm total}(Q) = \mu_{\rm proxy} \cdot \mu_{\rm baseline} + \mu_{\rm eff}(Q)$. The Halcyon coupling enters only through $\mu_{\rm eff}(Q)$, computed on the buckyball substrate via
\begin{equation}

\label{eq:mu-eff-discrete}

with $\kappa_Q(e) = (1 - q_0(U_{f_1(e)}))(1 - q_0(U_{f_2(e)}))$, $\tau_Q(e) = \tau_0 / (1 + \beta_\tau s_Q(e))$, and $\phi_n(e)$ the $n$-th non-trivial eigenmode of the buckyball graph Laplacian projected to edges via gradient. The model is material-independent by construction: $\mu_{\rm proxy}$ modulates the bare baseline only, so a non-zero $\partial_Q \mu_{\rm total} / \partial \mu_{\rm proxy}$ would indicate that what the simulation measures is a material artefact, not Halcyon. This is the $H_1$ test, and it discriminates because the model is designed to fail it cleanly if $\mu_{\rm proxy}$ were allowed to enter $\mu_{\rm eff}$ (an earlier draft of the SPEC made that mistake; the closure of v1's $H_1$ blocker is the load-bearing fix that earned the v2 design lock).

The marginal $H_7$ is therefore a faithful signal that the \texttt{--battery-fast} mode bumps into the limit of its own extractor, not that the signal is statistical noise --- the actual signal-to-noise on $\alpha$ is $|\alpha|/\sigma = 15.62$. The battery operates within the microcanonical energy shell sampled by the seed-and-canonical-init protocol; a \texttt{PASS\_SIMULATION\_ONLY} verdict therefore does not imply thermodynamic equilibrium. \subsection*{A.7.1 The full-battery follow-up: an honest falsifier of the extractor itself}

The smoke results above used a slope-through-origin extractor on the internal \texttt{mu\_eff\_mean} value tracked by the simulation. That number is the model's own internal state, not the value the apparatus would observe. SPEC v2 \S4 specifies a stricter protocol --- the H$_6$ test: fit $\chi_Q(\omega) = [K_Q + i c_Q \omega - \mu_Q \omega^2]^{-1}$ per $Q$ to the lock-in-demodulated $|\chi|$ and $\arg\chi$ curves at multiple $\omega$, then extract $\alpha$ from the fitted $\mu_Q$ values. The fitted $\mu_Q$ is what a real experiment would see; the internal $\mu_{\rm eff}(U(t))$ is what only the simulator knows. The two extractors are different epistemic objects, and the SPEC's choice to make the observation-space extractor load-bearing was the right one.

A full-battery run with the H$_6$ extractor wired up (sidecar \texttt{battery\_full\_20260620\_181227.json}, SHA-256 \texttt{ad33fd568f6257ff0ba98fd9d31a22403cb9915de083a6b6ca1384c49e4b49b8}, 111.2\,min wall, 5 $\omega$ $\times$ 3 $Q$ $\times$ 8 seeds main grid plus H$_1$/H$_5$/H$_9$ supplements) returned:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Sector separation: \textsc{pass} (17.83\,$\sigma$ minimum pairwise).
    \item Per-$Q$ measured $\chi(\omega)$ curves are \emph{identical to four decimal places across $Q$}: at $\omega = \omega_c$, $|\chi|_{Q=0} = 6.0519$, $|\chi|_{Q=1} = 6.0519$, $|\chi|_{Q=2} = 6.0522$. The Halcyon-predicted shift at the SPEC's default coupling $\alpha_{\rm Halcyon} = 1.0$ (lattice units) is roughly $60\,$ppm of the baseline susceptibility --- \emph{below the measurement noise floor of the observation-space extractor}.
    \item Sudoku verdict: \textsc{fail\_signal\_missing}. $H_0$ does not strike: $|\alpha_{\rm fitted}|/\sigma_\alpha = 0.21$, dominated by fit noise. The chapter does not claim a signal here.
    \item $H_1$ (material): \textsc{strike} (rel slope 0.0046; geometric independence robust under this extractor too).
    \item $H_5$ (amplitude linearity): \textsc{strike} (rel slope $\chi$ vs $F_0$ effectively 0).
    \item $H_6$ ($\chi(\omega)$ fit quality): \textsc{not struck} ($\chi^2/\rm dof = 1.3\times 10^8$; the fit explodes because the curves cannot discriminate three $Q$ values to within their dispersion).

\textbf{The interpretation.} The smoke-mode result was \emph{internally} consistent but \emph{externally} unmeasurable at the SPEC's default coupling. The two extractors give different verdicts because they ask different questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{extract\_alpha} (slope through origin on internal $\mu_{\rm eff}$): ``Does the model's own state respond to $Q$?'' --- answer \textsc{yes} at $|\alpha|/\sigma = 15.62$.
    \item \texttt{extract\_alpha\_from\_fits} (per-$Q$ $\chi(\omega)$ fit on observed susceptibility): ``Would a lock-in measuring the test mass see the response?'' --- answer \textsc{no} at $|\alpha|/\sigma = 0.21$.

\subsection*{A.6 The thread experiment and the dynamic transfer-function target}

The textbook two-thread inertia demonstration \cite[Vol.~I, \S10--2]{feynman_lectures} is the simplest geometry that already distinguishes the two readings of $\mu$ Halcyon needs to separate: $\mu$-as-a-property versus $\mu$-as-a-coupling. A weight hangs from a thread tied to the ceiling; a second thread hangs from the bottom of the weight to a hand. Pull the lower thread slowly and the upper thread breaks (it bears $mg + F$ in static equilibrium). Pull the lower thread fast and the lower thread breaks (the mass cannot redistribute the impulse fast enough; the lower thread bears $F$ alone). Same weight, same gravity, different breaking thread. The control parameter is the Deborah number \cite{reiner1964}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D} \;=\; \frac{\tau}{t_{\mathrm{load}}} \;=\; \frac{r\,\tau}{F_*}
\end{equation}
where $r=\mathrm{d}F/\mathrm{d}t$ is the loading rate, $t_{\mathrm{load}}=F_*/r$, $F_*$ is the breaking-force scale, and $\tau$ is the bulk's inertial relaxation time. The thread experiment lives in the two limits $\mathcal{D}\ll 1$ (quasi-static, upper bears $mg+F$) and $\mathcal{D}\gg 1$ (impulsive, lower bears $F$ alone).

\textbf{The idealised inertial crossover.} In an elastic model with no dissipation, the crossover from quasi-static to inertial response is set by the balance $K \sim \mu\,\omega^{2}$, giving
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{c} \;=\; \sqrt{K/\mu}, \qquad \tau_{\mu} \;=\; \sqrt{\mu/K}, \qquad \mu \;=\; K\,\tau_{\mu}^{2}.
\label{eq:inertial-crossover}
\end{equation}
The dimensional discipline matters: $\tau_{\mu}$ is the \emph{inertial} relaxation time (the time for an impulse to propagate through the bulk), not the viscous relaxation time $\tau_{\eta}=\eta/K$. Conflating the two is a common error; the thread demo, having no viscous element in the idealisation, isolates $\tau_{\mu}$ cleanly.

\textbf{The real apparatus.} For finite damping, the linear-response (Kubo) susceptibility for the single relevant degree of freedom \cite[\S6.4]{goldstein_classical_mechanics} is
\begin{equation}
    \chi_{Q}(\omega) \;=\; \frac{1}{K_{Q} + i\,c_{Q}\,\omega - \mu_{Q}\,\omega^{2}}.
\end{equation}
For a measured normal mode of shape $\phi_{n}(x)$, the mode-effective inertial coefficient is
\begin{equation}
    \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(n)}(Q) \;\sim\; \int \kappa_{Q}(x)\,\tau_{Q}^{2}(x)\,\lvert \phi_{n}(x)\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}V.
\label{eq:mode-effective-mass}
\end{equation}
The $|\phi_{n}|^{2}$ projection makes the coordinate dependence explicit; effective mass is mode-dependent in any extended elastic system.

\textbf{The falsifiable claim.} Halcyon's hypothesis, written cleanly:
\begin{equation}
    \partial_{Q}\,\mu_{Q} \;\stackrel{?}{\ne}\; 0
\label{eq:halcyon-claim}
\end{equation}
at fixed $K_{Q}$, $c_{Q}$, drive amplitude, thermal drift, and EM systematics. Three implementation discipline points:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Fit the full transfer function.} A shift in $\omega_{c}(Q)=\sqrt{K_{Q}/\mu_{Q}}$ alone proves only a shift in the ratio $K_{Q}/\mu_{Q}$. The clean inertial-shift claim requires fitting $K_{Q}$, $\mu_{Q}$, $c_{Q}$ as independent parameters.
\item \textit{Include damping.} The idealised model can omit $c_{Q}$; the apparatus model cannot. Mechanical mounts, eddy currents, and air loading all contribute finite damping that must be either physically suppressed or fit and subtracted.
\item \textit{Preregister systematics.} Drive amplitude, thermal drift, magnetic pickup, vibration, and cage drift constitute the systematics budget. Eq.~\ref{eq:halcyon-claim} is a positive claim only after that budget is set in advance and the residual slope exceeds it. This is the same discipline that resolved MICROSCOPE \cite{microscope2022}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The equivalence-principle framing.} This is an equivalence-principle-sensitive measurement, not an equivalence-principle-preserving one. MICROSCOPE bounds $m_{i}/m_{g}$ at the $10^{-15}$ level between materials at fixed material state \cite{microscope2022}; Halcyon tests whether $\mu_{Q}$ depends on the programmed gauge sector of the surrounding apparatus at fixed material and fixed static gravitational load. A non-zero $\partial_{Q}\mu_{Q}$ is an EP-sensitive result --- not a contradiction-free preservation of $m_{i}=m_{g}$. Halcyon is by construction a \emph{state-programmed equivalence-principle stress test}: same test mass, same gravitational load, different programmed gauge sector.

The scale never notices. A weight measurement is the wrong instrument; a lock-in driven response measurement at multiple frequencies, fitted across multiple $Q$, is the right one.
